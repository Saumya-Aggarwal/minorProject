# Customer discovery interviews: online D2C brands

**Goal:** find out, before building anything more, whether online ethnic-wear brands
have a WhatsApp sales problem that is painful enough to pilot, and later pay for, Munim.

**Hypothesis being tested:** *a Shopify / WooCommerce add-on for online ethnic-wear
brands. It answers customers on WhatsApp with prices and stock taken from the brand's
real store, keeps one cart across WhatsApp and the website, and handles wedding and
family orders (several people, several functions, one cart).*

We are not selling or demoing in these calls. We are learning how they work today.
Method: *The Mom Test* (Rob Fitzpatrick). Ask about what they did in the past, not what they would do.

---

## 1. Who to talk to

| | |
|---|---|
| **Target** | 10–15 online brands: ethnic wear first, plus 2–3 from another category (general fashion, jewellery, beauty) to compare |
| **Person** | The founder, or whoever answers WhatsApp / Instagram chats and handles orders |
| **Good fit** | Has a website (Shopify or WooCommerce) **and** sells or answers questions on WhatsApp / Instagram |
| **Length** | 20–30 minutes, phone or video |

**Where to find them**
- Instagram: brands you follow, and brands tagged in wedding-outfit posts and reels
- LinkedIn: search "founder" + "ethnic wear" / "D2C" / "Shopify"
- College incubator or entrepreneurship cell: ask for founder introductions
- Shopify India / D2C founder groups (LinkedIn, WhatsApp communities, Discord)
- At the end of every call, ask: *"Who else should I talk to?"*

## 2. Outreach messages

**Instagram DM / WhatsApp**
> Hi! I'm Saumya, a student building an AI assistant for WhatsApp shopping. I'm not
> selling anything. I'm trying to understand how brands like yours handle customer
> chats and orders on WhatsApp today. Could I ask you 20 minutes of questions this
> week? Happy to share what I learn from other brands.

**LinkedIn / email**
> Subject: 20 minutes on how you handle WhatsApp orders?
>
> Hi <name>, I'm a final-year student working on conversational commerce for Indian
> D2C brands. I'm speaking with founders to understand how customer chats on
> WhatsApp and Instagram turn into orders today, and what breaks. No pitch, only
> questions. Would you have 20 minutes this week or next? I'll share a summary of
> what I hear across brands.

Follow up once after 3–4 days. Don't send a third message.

---

## 3. The call

**Opening (1 min)**
> Thanks for your time. I'm not selling anything today. I want to understand how
> you handle customer chats and orders. There are no right answers, and "we don't
> do that" is a useful answer. Mind if I take notes?

### A. How it works today
1. Walk me through the last time a customer messaged you on WhatsApp or Instagram before buying. What happened, step by step?
2. Roughly how many of those chats do you get a day? In wedding season?
3. Who answers them, and how long does that take them in a day?
4. What tools do you use: WhatsApp Business app, Interakt / Wati / AiSensy / others, Meta's Business AI, Shopify Inbox?
5. How does a chat become an order? Do you send a website link, a payment link, or take the order by hand?

### B. Where it hurts
6. What do customers ask most? (sizes, stock, "something for a wedding", COD, delivery date, returns)
7. What goes wrong? (slow replies, missed chats, wrong size, returns, abandoned carts, fake COD orders)
8. When did you last lose a sale because nobody replied in time or gave the wrong answer? How did you find out?
9. What have you already tried to fix it? Why did it work or not?

### C. Wedding and family orders (tests the headline feature)
10. Do you get orders for several people at once, like a whole family for a wedding or matching outfits for a function?
11. How do you handle them today? (sizes per person, colour matching, split payments, delivery dates)
12. How big is such an order compared to a normal one?

### D. Money and trust
13. What do you pay for WhatsApp / chat tools now? Was it worth it?
14. Have you tried an AI chatbot? What happened? Did it ever say something wrong to a customer, like a price, stock or a promise?
15. What would an AI have to get right before you let it answer your customers alone?

### E. Close
16. Is there anything I should have asked but didn't?
17. If I build something for this, can I show you in about two weeks? Would you try it with your real catalogue?
18. Who else should I talk to?

---

## 4. Rules during the call

- **Ask about the past, not the future.** "When did that last happen?" beats "would you use...?".
- **Don't pitch.** If they ask what you're building, answer in one line and go back to your questions.
- **Get numbers.** Chats per day, hours spent, order value, rupees paid for tools.
- **Dig into emotion.** When they sound frustrated, ask "tell me more about that".
- **Compliments are not data.** "Sounds cool" means nothing. A commitment means something.
- **Listen more than you talk,** roughly 80/20.

## 6. Scoring and decision

Score each call right after it:

| Signal | Points |
|---|---|
| Already pays for a WhatsApp / chat tool | +1 |
| Described a specific lost sale or failure in the last month | +1 |
| Spends 2+ hours a day on chats | +1 |
| Gets wedding / family multi-person orders regularly | +1 |
| Offered their catalogue or a pilot | +2 |
| Named a price they would pay | +2 |

**Decision after 10–15 calls:**
- **Go:** 3 or more brands agree to pilot with their real catalogue, and the pain they describe isn't something Meta's free Business AI already solves.
- **Pivot:** the pain is real but elsewhere (for example COD confirmation, returns, catalogue creation). Rewrite the hypothesis and do 5 more calls.
- **Stop:** nobody commits to anything beyond "interesting".
